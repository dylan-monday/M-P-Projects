# Monday + Partners — Sprint Services
## Productized, AI-First Creative Sprints

_Business model + system spec · March 2026_

---

## The Concept

Monday + Partners Sprint Services is a productized offering: fixed-scope, fixed-price creative projects delivered in 2–4 weeks using AI-first workflows. Clients select a project type, complete a guided intake, receive a proposal with clear deliverables and pricing, approve and pay, and get their project delivered through the same portal system already being built.

This is not a custom agency engagement. It's a menu. The constraints are the product: defined scope, defined timeline, defined price, defined deliverables. That's what makes it scalable — and what makes AI workflows viable as the primary production method.

### Why This Works for M+P

- **Leverages your AI-first toolchain.** Claude Code, MCP tools, and your existing design/dev skills mean you can deliver $10–15K worth of agency output in 20–30 hours of actual work.
- **Low overhead, high margin.** No scope creep (scope is pre-defined). No estimate negotiation (prices are fixed). No project management bloat (milestones are templatized).
- **Launchpad to bigger relationships.** A $5K site rebuild becomes a $50K brand engagement once they see the quality. Sprint projects are the top of the funnel.
- **Recurring capability.** Once the templates, workflows, and intake system exist, each new project is faster than the last. The 10th website sprint takes half the time of the first.
- **Capitalizes on market timing.** Small businesses know they need better digital presence but can't afford traditional agency pricing. "Premium quality, sprint timeline, accessible price" is an underserved position.

## Sprint Project Types

### What qualifies as a Sprint project:

- Fixed, well-defined deliverable (not ongoing or open-ended)
- Can be scoped and estimated from a structured brief (no discovery phase needed)
- Deliverable is primarily digital (websites, landing pages, brand systems, presentations, etc.)
- Can be completed in 2–4 weeks with AI-assisted workflows
- Falls in the $5,000–$15,000 range
- Client provides necessary inputs (content, brand assets, access) within the first week

### Proposed Sprint Menu

**1. Website Rebuild / Redesign — $7,500–$12,500**
_What they get:_ Modern, fast, responsive website (up to 8 pages). Next.js or static. Clean design, mobile-first, deployed and live.
_What they provide:_ Current site URL, brand assets (logo, colors), content direction, 3–5 reference sites they like.
_Timeline:_ 3–4 weeks
_Milestones:_ Brief → Design Direction → Build → Review → Launch
_Example:_ The LGM/PPP project (minus the shared ecosystem and portal — that's custom scope)

**2. Landing Page / Campaign Page — $5,000–$7,500**
_What they get:_ Single high-impact page. Animated, conversion-optimized, with form/CTA integration. Deployed and live.
_What they provide:_ Campaign brief, brand assets, target audience, desired action.
_Timeline:_ 2–3 weeks
_Milestones:_ Brief → Design → Build → Review → Launch

**3. Brand Identity Sprint — $7,500–$10,000**
_What they get:_ Logo system, color palette, typography, brand guidelines document (PDF), basic asset kit (social templates, email signature, business card layout).
_What they provide:_ Business description, target audience, competitive landscape, aesthetic preferences.
_Timeline:_ 3–4 weeks
_Milestones:_ Brief → Concepts (2–3 directions) → Refinement → Final Delivery

**4. Brand Essence Discovery — $5,000–$7,500**
_What they get:_ 3 × 30-minute stakeholder interviews (AI-transcribed and analyzed), a synthesized brand truth document identifying core themes, differentiators, and messaging opportunities. A strategic foundation to build on — not executional work, but the insight layer beneath it.
_What they provide:_ 3 stakeholder names/contacts, background on the business, any existing brand or positioning materials.
_Timeline:_ 2–3 weeks
_Milestones:_ Brief → Interviews Scheduled → Interviews Complete → Synthesis → Delivery
_Notes:_ Interviews conducted by Dylan via video call. AI handles transcription, thematic analysis, and first-pass synthesis. Dylan edits and presents the final document. This Sprint is often a precursor to a Brand Identity Sprint or a larger engagement.

**5. Brand Audit — $5,000–$7,500**
_What they get:_ A thorough review of current branding (visual identity, messaging, digital presence, competitive positioning) delivered as a 10-slide response: what's working, what's not, and where the opportunities are. Clear, actionable, designed.
_What they provide:_ Access to current brand materials, website URL, social accounts, any existing guidelines.
_Timeline:_ 2–3 weeks
_Milestones:_ Brief → Audit Research → Draft Deck → Review → Final Delivery
_Notes:_ This is a diagnostic, not a rebrand. The output tells the client where they stand and what to fix. Often leads to a Brand Identity Sprint or Website Rebuild.

**6. Brand Voice + Content System — $5,000–$10,000**
_What they get:_ Brand voice guide, messaging framework, 5–10 sample content pieces (social posts, email templates, ad copy), and optionally a trained AI content assistant (BrandVoice-style).
_What they provide:_ Existing brand materials, target audience, tone preferences, content channels.
_Timeline:_ 2–3 weeks
_Milestones:_ Brief → Voice Framework → Content Samples → Review → Delivery

**7. Email / Nurture Sequence — $5,000–$7,500**
_What they get:_ Designed, written email sequence (5–8 emails). Templates built in their ESP (Mailchimp, SendGrid, etc.). Strategy + copy + design.
_What they provide:_ Audience, goals, brand assets, ESP access.
_Timeline:_ 2–3 weeks
_Milestones:_ Brief → Strategy → Copy + Design → Review → Deployment

**8. Custom Tool Deployment — $5,000–$10,000**
_What they get:_ A configured, branded instance of one of M+P's AI-developed internal tools — deployed for the client's use. Examples: timesheet tracker, project dashboard, content calendar, client reporting tool. Includes customization (branding, fields, workflow), deployment, and onboarding.
_What they provide:_ Requirements, team size, workflow description, brand assets.
_Timeline:_ 2–3 weeks
_Milestones:_ Brief → Configuration → Deployment → Training/Onboarding → Handoff
_Notes:_ This only applies to tools M+P has already built. We're not building custom software from scratch in a Sprint — we're configuring and deploying an existing tool. If the client needs something that doesn't exist yet, that's a custom engagement.

### What's NOT a Sprint project:
- Ongoing retainers or monthly services
- Projects requiring extensive discovery, research, or strategy phases (Brand Essence Discovery is the exception — it's scoped tightly around 3 interviews)
- E-commerce builds with complex inventory/payment systems
- Projects where the client can't provide necessary inputs in week 1
- Anything where scope is ambiguous or "we'll figure it out as we go"
- Video production, photography, or physical media
- **Presentation design** — too open-ended, too dependent on client content iteration, scope creep magnet
- Custom software development (deploying existing tools is fine; building new ones is not)
- Projects over $15,000 (those become custom engagements through a different path)

### AI-Guided Intake Form

When the client selects a project type, they enter a conversational intake — not a static form. This is where the Anthropic API integration lives.

**The experience:**
- Starts with 3–5 structured fields (name, email, phone, company, website URL)
- Then transitions into a guided conversation specific to the project type
- The AI asks clarifying questions based on their answers — not a fixed sequence, but an intelligent flow
- Questions are pre-scripted per project type but the AI can probe deeper on ambiguous answers
- The AI assesses feasibility against the Sprint parameters (scope, timeline, budget) in real-time
- If the project fits, the intake confirms: "This looks like a great fit for our [Project Type] Sprint. We'll review your brief and get back within 1 business day with a proposal."
- If the project doesn't fit (too complex, too vague, too large), the AI explains why and suggests alternatives: "This sounds like it might need a custom engagement. Let's schedule a call to discuss."

**Per-project-type intake questions (examples):**

Website Rebuild:
- What's your current website URL?
- What's the main problem with your current site? (Looks dated / Doesn't convert / Hard to update / All of the above)
- How many pages do you need? (1–3 / 4–8 / 8+)
- Do you have brand assets? (Logo, colors, fonts)
- Share 2–3 websites you admire (URLs)
- What's the primary action you want visitors to take? (Contact form / Call / Purchase / Book)
- Do you have your content ready, or do you need help with copy?
- Timeline: when do you need this live?

Brand Identity:
- Describe your business in 1–2 sentences
- Who is your target audience?
- Name 2–3 competitors
- Describe your brand personality in 3 adjectives
- Any colors, styles, or aesthetics you're drawn to?
- Any colors, styles, or aesthetics you want to avoid?
- Do you have an existing logo or visual identity?
- What will this brand identity be used on first? (Website / Business cards / Social / Signage)

Brand Essence Discovery:
- Describe your business and what you offer
- What's prompting this? (New brand / Rebrand / Messaging refresh / Strategic clarity)
- Who are the 3 stakeholders we'd interview? (Names, roles, email addresses)
- What do you think your brand stands for today?
- What do you wish it stood for?
- Any existing brand materials, positioning docs, or messaging we should review?

Brand Audit:
- What's your current website URL?
- Social media accounts (links)
- Do you have brand guidelines? (Upload or link if yes)
- What feels wrong about your current brand? (Looks dated / Inconsistent / Doesn't reflect who we are / Not sure)
- Who are your top 2–3 competitors?
- What would success look like from this audit?

Custom Tool Deployment:
- Which tool are you interested in? (Timesheet Tracker / Project Dashboard / Content Calendar / Other)
- How many team members will use it?
- Describe your current workflow for this
- What's not working about your current process?
- Do you have brand assets for customization? (Logo, colors)

**Technical implementation:**
- The intake page uses the Anthropic API (Claude) with a system prompt that:
  - Knows the Sprint project types, scopes, and parameters
  - Has a question bank per project type
  - Assesses feasibility as the conversation progresses
  - Flags anything that falls outside Sprint parameters
  - Summarizes the brief at the end for Dylan's review
- The conversation is stored in Supabase (new table: `intake_sessions`)
- On completion, an email notification goes to Dylan with the structured brief
- The client sees: "We'll review your brief and send a proposal within 1 business day."

### What Happens After Intake — Admin Review to Proposal

1. **Dylan receives notification** with the structured brief (email + visible in admin dashboard at `/admin/intake`).
2. **Dylan reviews the intake** in the admin dashboard (5–10 minutes):
   - Reads the AI-structured brief
   - Sees the full conversation transcript
   - Sees the AI's feasibility assessment and any flags
   - Decides: **Convert to project** / **Request more info** / **Decline**
3. **If converting:** Dylan clicks "Create Proposal" which:
   - Pre-fills a proposal editor with the Sprint template for that project type
   - Populates client details, brief summary, and project-specific notes from the intake
   - Sets default milestones, timeline, and pricing for the Sprint type
   - Dylan can edit any field: adjust pricing, customize scope, add/remove deliverables, add personal notes
   - Dylan can preview the proposal exactly as the client will see it
4. **Dylan publishes the proposal** — this triggers:
   - Stripe products/prices created automatically via API
   - Project record created in Supabase
   - Client record created (or linked if existing)
   - Proposal email sent to client: "Your proposal is ready. View it here: [link]"
5. **Client logs in, reads proposal, reviews terms, e-signs, and pays deposit.** (See Terms & E-Signature section below.)
6. **Project proceeds through milestones.** Same portal, same dashboard.

### The "Not Sure" Path

If the client selects "Not sure — tell us what you need," the AI intake acts as a general creative brief tool:
- Asks what they're trying to accomplish
- Asks about budget range
- Asks about timeline
- Based on answers, either routes them to the appropriate Sprint type or suggests a custom consultation
- If it's clearly outside Sprint scope: "This sounds like a bigger engagement — let's schedule a 15-minute call to discuss." → Cal.com link

## Proposal Templates

Each Sprint type gets a proposal template stored in the system. When Dylan creates a project from a Sprint intake, the template auto-populates with:

- Client name and company (from intake)
- Project-specific details (from intake conversation)
- Standard scope for that Sprint type
- Standard milestones
- Pricing (from the Sprint tier, adjustable by Dylan)
- Standard terms and timeline

**Dylan's role is curation, not creation.** Review the auto-generated proposal, adjust anything that needs it, and send. 15–30 minutes per proposal instead of 3–4 hours.

### Template structure per Sprint type:

```
1. Hero: "Your [Project Type] Sprint"
2. The Brief: Summary of what the client described (pulled from intake)
3. What You Get: Deliverables list (from template, adjusted if needed)
4. How It Works: Timeline + milestones (from template)
5. Investment: Price + payment terms (50% deposit / 50% on completion)
6. About M+P: Brief credibility section
7. Terms & Conditions: Full standard terms (expandable) + PDF download
8. Accept: Checkbox agreement + typed signature + "Accept & Pay Deposit" button
```

Simpler than the LGM/PPP proposal (which is a full cinematic pitch). Sprint proposals are clean, direct, and fast to read. The client already expressed interest through the intake — the proposal confirms scope and price, it doesn't need to sell the concept.

## Terms & E-Signature

### Standard Terms Integration

M+P's Standard Terms and Conditions (see `new_terms.pdf`) are legally binding and govern every project. They cover: payment (50/50), cancellation/kill fee, work product ownership (work for hire with license-back on M+P pre-existing IP), scope changes, client responsibilities, delays, limitation of liability, and Louisiana governing law.

**How terms are presented in the portal:**

1. The proposal includes a "Terms & Conditions" section (not buried — clearly visible as a distinct section before the payment CTA)
2. The full terms are displayed inline (expandable/collapsible) or as a clearly linked PDF download
3. The terms are NOT editable by the client — they're standard. If a client wants custom terms, that's a conversation, not a portal feature.

### E-Signature Flow

When the client is ready to accept the proposal and pay the deposit, the flow is:

```
[Client reads proposal]
        ↓
[Scrolls to Accept section]
        ↓
[Terms displayed — expandable full text + "Download Terms PDF" link]
        ↓
[Checkbox: "I, [Client Name], agree to the Monday + Partners
 Standard Terms and Conditions and the project scope
 described in this proposal."]
        ↓
[Typed signature field: "Type your full legal name"]
        ↓
[Accept & Pay Deposit — $X,XXX]
        ↓
[Stripe Checkout → payment → return to portal]
```

### What Gets Recorded

On acceptance, the system stores in Supabase:

```sql
-- Add to projects table or a new agreements table
agreed_to_terms boolean default false,
agreed_at timestamptz,
signer_name text,                    -- typed full legal name
signer_email text,                   -- from auth session
signer_ip text,                      -- captured at time of agreement
terms_version text,                  -- "v1.0" — tracks which version they signed
```

This creates a legally defensible record: who agreed, when, from what IP, to which version of the terms. The Stripe payment receipt serves as additional evidence of the transaction.

### Terms Versioning

If M+P's terms ever change, the system tracks which version each client signed. Old projects retain their original terms. New proposals reference the current version. The `terms_version` field in the database links to the specific terms document in Supabase Storage.

### W-9

M+P's W-9 is on file (EIN: 86-1612925, Monday and Partners LLC, S-Corp). If a client requests a W-9 (common for businesses paying >$600), it can be downloaded from the portal or sent automatically as part of the onboarding email. Store the current W-9 PDF in Supabase Storage and make it available via admin.

## System Architecture (additions to existing portal spec)

### New Supabase Tables

```sql
-- Sprint project types (the menu)
create table sprint_types (
  id uuid primary key default gen_random_uuid(),
  slug text unique not null,          -- "website-rebuild"
  title text not null,                -- "Website Rebuild / Redesign"
  description text,
  price_min integer,                  -- cents: 750000
  price_max integer,                  -- cents: 1250000
  default_price integer,              -- cents: 1000000
  timeline_weeks integer,             -- 3
  intake_system_prompt text,          -- AI prompt for this project type's intake
  intake_questions jsonb,             -- structured question bank
  proposal_template jsonb,            -- template sections for auto-generated proposals
  default_milestones jsonb,           -- ["Brief", "Design Direction", "Build", "Review", "Launch"]
  active boolean default true,
  sort_order integer default 0
);

-- Intake sessions (conversations from the public form)
create table intake_sessions (
  id uuid primary key default gen_random_uuid(),
  sprint_type_id uuid references sprint_types(id),
  client_name text,
  client_email text,
  client_phone text,
  client_company text,
  client_website text,
  conversation jsonb,                 -- full conversation history
  structured_brief jsonb,             -- AI-generated summary of the brief
  feasibility text,                   -- "fit" | "maybe" | "custom"
  status text default 'pending',      -- pending | reviewed | converted | declined
  project_id uuid references projects(id),  -- linked if converted to a project
  created_at timestamptz default now()
);
```

## What Needs to Be True for This to Work

1. **Sprint types are well-defined.** The menu needs to be tight. Each type has a clear scope, clear deliverables, and clear boundaries. If a client asks for something outside the box, the intake catches it and routes to custom.

2. **Intake AI is well-prompted.** The system prompt per Sprint type needs to be carefully crafted — it's doing qualification, scoping, and brief-building simultaneously. This is a prompt engineering project in itself. Worth investing time here.

3. **Templates are good enough to send with minimal edits.** The proposal templates need to be 80% done before Dylan touches them. If every proposal requires a full rewrite, the model breaks.

4. **AI workflows are genuinely faster.** The margin comes from speed. If a Website Rebuild Sprint takes 60 hours of actual work, the pricing doesn't hold. It needs to be 20–30 hours of Dylan's time (strategy, design direction, QA, client communication) with AI handling the production.

5. **Client inputs arrive on time.** The biggest risk to a 3–4 week timeline is waiting for client assets. The intake and onboarding flow need to make this crystal clear: "We need [X] by [date] or the timeline shifts."

6. **The portfolio grows.** Each completed Sprint becomes a showcase piece. After 5–10 projects, the Sprint page has a portfolio that sells the next one.

## Pricing Strategy

### Sprint Pricing Principles
- **Fixed price, not hourly.** The client knows what they're paying before they start.
- **Tiered within each Sprint type.** The range (e.g., $7,500–$12,500 for a website) allows Dylan to adjust based on complexity surfaced during intake.
- **50/50 payment structure.** Deposit on approval, final on completion. Same as the portal already supports.
- **No scope creep mechanism.** The proposal defines the deliverables. Anything outside that scope is a separate Sprint or a custom engagement. This is stated in the terms.
- **Revision policy.** 2 rounds of revision included. Additional rounds billed at a flat rate. Stated in the proposal.

### What Dylan actually earns
If a Website Rebuild Sprint costs the client $10,000 and takes Dylan 25 hours of actual work (with AI handling production), that's $400/hour effective rate. Even at 40 hours, it's $250/hour. Compare to:
- Traditional agency: bills 80–120 hours at $150–200/hour for the same output
- Freelance creative director: $150–250/hour, but projects drag and scope creeps

The Sprint model works because the scope is fixed, the AI does the heavy lifting, and Dylan's time is spent on the decisions that matter — not the execution.
